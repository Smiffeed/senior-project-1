Chapter 1: Introduction
1.1 Background
Content moderation remains a critical challenge for digital media platforms which contain hundreds of explicit contents, especially in languages with limited computational resources such as Thai. While automated profanity detection systems are well-research for languages like English for text detection (Davidson, Warmsley, Macy, & Weber, 2017), and audio detection (Wazir, et al., 2022). Thai presents unique computational challenges due to its tonal nature, complex phonetic structure (Theera-Umpon, Chansareewittaya, & Auephanwiriyakul, 2011), and the scarcity of large-scale profanity-labeled datasets. Moreover, traditional manual censorship is still widely used, but it is time-consuming, inconsistent, and impractical for large-scale content moderation.
Recent advances in speech recognition and deep learning have enabled more sophisticated content moderation tools. However, most existing models are trained on non-Thai datasets, resulting in high error rates when applied to Thai profanity detection. Our work addressed these challenges by fine-tuning a pre-trained Wav2Vec2 model (Baevski, Zhou, Mohamed, & Auli, 2020) specifically for Thai profanity detection through supervised learning with sequence classification. The base model used was airesearch/wav2vec2-large-xlsr-53-th, which had been pre-trained on Thai speech data using self-supervised learning techniques.

While this initial system demonstrated promising results, its performance was constrained by limited training data availability, with restricted time and computational resources. The fundamental issue in low-resource language processing is that deep learning models typically require extensive labeled datasets to achieve optimal performance, which may be impractical to obtain for specialized tasks like profanity detection (Kenton, Devlin, Change, & Toutanova, 2019)
1.2 Problem Statement
The development of robust Thai profanity detection models faces a fundamental constraint: the lack of extensive labeled datasets that would typically be required for optimal performance. While ASR-based solutions show promise, they struggle with Thai's phonetic diversity, slang variations, and tonal ambiguities, leading to inconsistent profanity detection accuracy. More critically, the evaluation methodologies used to assess these systems often lack systematic analysis of temporal parameters specifically window size and stride configurations that significantly impact detection performance in practical deployment scenarios.

Traditional evaluation approaches in speech processing typically use fixed parameters without exploring the relationship between temporal segmentation settings and detection accuracy. In profanity detection, the choice of analysis window size and stride directly affects both detection sensitivity and temporal precision, yet existing evaluation methods rarely provide appropriate stride for parameter optimization (Wazir, et al., 2022). Especially, Thai language which has difference in phoneme and tone.

Recognizing the data limitation as an inherent challenge rather than a solvable problem within the scope of our research, this project shifts focus from purely model development to comprehensive evaluation methodology and parameter optimization. The central question becomes: Given limited training data, how can we systematically identify the most effective evaluation approaches and optimal system parameters (window size and stride configurations) to guide future research and practical implementation?
1.3 Scope of the Project
This project centers on the comprehensive evaluation and optimization of Thai profanity detection systems in audio content. Building upon the foundational fine-tuned Wav2Vec2 model developed in Senior Project 1, we design and implement multiple evaluation methodologies to assess detection performance and meaningful evaluation results.
The research systematically explores the impact of critical temporal parameters. Specifically, window size (ranging from 0.3s to 2.0s) and stride configurations (in percentage of window length and changing stride every 0.05 seconds) on detection accuracy across different parameter combinations.
1.4 Significance
This research addresses a critical practical need in Thai profanity detection by systematically identifying the optimal window and stride parameters for different evaluation methods. The primary significance lies in providing clear, evidence-based recommendations for future researchers and practitioners working on similar Thai language processing tasks, eliminating the need for time-consuming trial-and-error parameter selection.
The project's main contributions include:
1. Optimal Parameter Identification: Through systematic analysis of window and stride combinations across evaluation method the study identifies the best-performing configurations for each evaluation approach, providing concrete guidance for future model implementations.
2. Evaluation System Framework: The comprehensive evaluation system developed can be directly applied by future researchers to assess their own Thai profanity detection models, ensuring consistent and reliable performance measurement across different implementations.
The practical impact of this work enables future developers to immediately implement optimal configurations (such as 0.3s windows for word-level tasks) rather than spending significant time on parameter exploration, accelerating the development of Thai language content moderation systems in large dataset.
Chapter 2: Related Work
Profanity detection in speech has been a significant research area in natural language processing (NLP) and speech processing. Existing methods generally fall into three main categories: Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)-based text processing (Jacobs, Rakotonirina, Chimoto, Bassett, & Kamper, 2023, An, et al., 2024), direct speech-based profanity detection using deep learning (An, et al., 2024, Wazir, et al., 2022), and text-based hate speech research (Hemtanon, Phetkrachang, & Yangyuen, 2024) or keyword-based approach (Aljumily, 2023). This section provides an overview of these approaches and their relevance to profanity detection in Thai speech, with particular emphasis on evaluation methodologies and parameter optimization techniques that inform our research approach.
2.1 Speech-Based Profanity Detection Approaches
2.1.1 ASR-Based Profanity Detection
Traditional approaches to profanity detection in speech typically involve a two-stage process: first converting speech to text using Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems, then applying text-based profanity detection algorithms (Davidson, Warmsley, Macy, & Weber, 2017). While this approach benefits from mature text processing techniques and extensive text-based profanity datasets, it is unable to locate timestamps in audio precisely.

2.1.2 Direct Speech-Based Profanity Detection
Recent advancements in deep learning have enabled direct processing of raw audio for speech classification, bypassing the need for ASR. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been applied to spectrogram representations of audio signals for emotion and profanity classification (Wazir, et al., 2022); . More recently, self-supervised learning models like wav2vec 2.0 (Baevski, Zhou, Mohamed, & Auli, 2020).

Wav2Vec2 architecture offers significant advantages over ASR-based methods for profanity detection through its direct waveform analysis capabilities. Unlike ASR systems that convert speech to discrete text tokens, Wav2Vec2 processes raw audio waveforms directly, enabling precise temporal localization of acoustic features (Baevski, Zhou, Mohamed, & Auli, 2020). This direct analysis approach allows for fine-grained windowing strategies that can capture the exact temporal boundaries of profane words, which is crucial for accurate detection and subsequent censoring applications. The model's ability to analyze speech at various temporal resolutions through configurable window sizes makes it particularly suitable for profanity detection tasks where precise temporal localization is essential for practical deployment.
2.2 Evaluation Methodologies in Speech Processing
2.2.1 Window Evaluation
A common methodology for analyzing continuous audio streams is window-based evaluation. This approach involves segmenting the audio into fixed-size windows, which are then individually classified.
A pertinent example is the work of Wazir et al. (2022), who investigated profane word detection for film censorship. They systematically analyzed the effect of window length by testing segments of 0.3, 0.4, and 0.5 seconds. Their findings indicated that model performance, measured by metrics such as F1-score and accuracy, improved with longer segments, leading them to conclude that 0.5 seconds was the optimal window length for their system.
Furthermore, Wazir et al. (2022) addressed the inherent problem of fixed windowing where words can be split across two segments. They introduced an "overlap time" (a stride of less than 100%) and demonstrated that using overlapping windows significantly improved detection accuracy by reducing false negatives.
While their study established a clear benefit for a specific window size and the use of an overlap, it did not perform a broad analysis of different stride values. This leaves an open question regarding the optimal relationship between window size and stride percentage, which is a central focus of our research.
2.2.2 Frame Evaluation
A more granular approach to audio event detection is frame-level evaluation, as demonstrated in the work on explainable hate speech detection by An et al. (2023). In this methodology, the audio signal is segmented into extremely small, fixed-size "frames," typically around 10 milliseconds.
Unlike window-based methods that classify a larger segment of audio, a frame-level model directly analyzes the raw audio waveform and makes an independent prediction for each individual frame. The primary goal is to generate a detailed, time-stamped sequence of classifications, often referred to as a "rationale," which pinpoints the exact temporal locations of the event of interest (e.g., hate speech).
The performance of this method is not just measured by classification accuracy but, more importantly, by localization metrics. An et al. (2023) utilize the Intersection over Union (IoU) metric, which evaluates how well the predicted sequence of positive frames overlaps with the ground-truth sequence. This makes frame-level evaluation particularly powerful for tasks requiring high temporal precision. However, this approach relies on having datasets with highly detailed, frame-by-frame annotations, which are often resource-intensive to create. This contrasts with our work, which investigates the optimal parameters for a window-based approach that is more practical for datasets without such fine-grained labels.

Both frame-level and window-based evaluation methodologies offer distinct advantages for speech processing tasks. Frame-level analysis, as demonstrated by An et al. (2023), provides unparalleled temporal precision, making it ideal for tasks requiring exact localization and explainability. However, its primary prerequisite is the availability of datasets with exhaustive, frame-by-frame annotations. Creating such detailed labels is extremely resource-intensive and often impractical, especially in the context of low-resource languages like Thai.
In contrast, window-based evaluation presents a robust and highly effective alternative that functions well with more commonly available datasets, which typically have word-level or clip-level labels. This approach aligns perfectly with the primary objective of this research: to determine the optimal temporal parameters for a practical profanity detection system.
Therefore, for this project, we have adopted window-based evaluation methodology. This strategic choice allows us to conduct a large-scale, systematic analysis of how different window and stride configurations impact detection performance, without being constrained by the need for fine-grained, frame-level annotated data. Our focus is on optimizing a practical and deployable system, making the window-based approach the most suitable and logical choice.
2.3 Challenges in Thai Speech Profanity Detection
2.3.1 Linguistic Characteristics
Thai presents unique computational challenges due to its tonal nature, complex phonetic structure, and lack of explicit word boundaries in written text. These characteristics directly impact speech processing systems, requiring specialized approaches for effective analysis. The tonal nature of Thai means that subtle variations in pitch can change word meanings, adding complexity to automatic processing systems and potentially affecting optimal parameter choices (Suwanbandit, 2024).
2.3.2 Phonetic Complexity and Parameter Impact
The complex phonetic structure of Thai, including its tonal characteristics and phoneme variations, suggests that optimal temporal parameters may differ significantly from those established for non-tonal languages (Theera-Umpon, Chansareewittaya, & Auephanwiriyakul, 2011). This linguistic specificity necessitates dedicated parameter optimization studies for Thai language applications rather than adopting configurations optimized for other languages.
2.3.3 Limited Dataset Availability
Thai language processing suffers from limited availability of large-scale labeled datasets, particularly for specialized tasks like profanity detection. This constraint necessitates efficient approaches to model development and evaluation that can provide maximum insight with minimal data requirements, making parameter optimization studies particularly valuable for maximizing the utility of available data.

3.4 Training Approach
3.4.1 Training Strategy
Our training methodology was carefully crafted to leverage the power of transfer learning while addressing the unique challenges of Thai profanity detection. We began with a pre-trained Wav2Vec 2.0 model airesearch/wav2vec2-large-xlsr-53-th, which had already learned rich representations from general speech recognition tasks in Thai Language. This foundation proved invaluable, as it provided our model with a sophisticated understanding of audio patterns and speech characteristics before we fine-tuned it for our specific use case.
The training process was meticulously configured to optimize learning while preventing overfitting. We settled on a batch size of 16 after extensive experimentation, finding this to be the sweet spot between computational efficiency and stable gradient updates. The learning rate was carefully tuned to 5e-5 with a linear decay schedule, allowing the model to make precise adjustments to its parameters without overshooting optimal values. This was particularly crucial given the nuanced nature of Thai pronunciation and the importance of capturing subtle acoustic differences between profane and non-profane utterances. 3.4.2 Hyperparameter Optimization
The core of this research is a large-scale, systematic investigation to identify the optimal data processing parameters for Thai profanity detection using a fine-tuned Wav2Vec2 model airesearch/wav2vec2-large-xlsr-53-th. Rather than focusing on model architecture hyperparameters, our work centers on the critical pre-processing stage that defines how the model "sees" the audio data: the windowing and stride configuration.
To achieve this, we designed and executed an exhaustive parameter sweep, evaluating over windows and strides unique combinations. This experiment was structured to explore the full landscape of temporal parameters:
Window Size: We tested a wide range of window lengths, from short 0.3-second segments up to 2.0-second segments, to understand the trade-offs between temporal precision and contextual understanding.
Stride Configuration: For each window size, we evaluated a comprehensive set of stride values. This included both percentage-based overlaps (from 10% to 90%) and fixed-time overlaps, allowing us to analyze the impact of varying degrees of redundancy between consecutive windows.
This extensive grid search was not evaluated against a single metric. Instead, each of the 1,200+ parameter combinations was assessed across four distinct evaluation methodologies: merged word evaluation, word iou evaluation, iou evaluation, and window evaluation. This multi-faceted approach ensures that our findings are robust and provides a nuanced understanding of how window and stride parameters affect different aspects of detection performance, from simple classification to precise temporal localization. The goal of this comprehensive experiment is to move beyond anecdotal evidence and provide a data-driven guide to the optimal parameters for this specific task.
3.5 Evaluation Methodology
Our evaluation framework is designed to be comprehensive and appropriate for real-world usage in censoring, assessing model performance across a wide range of parameters and through four distinct methodologies. A key aspect of our experiment is the investigation of two different striding strategies to process the audio data:
1. Fixed-Time Stride: A constant step size of 0.05 seconds is used between consecutive windows.
2. Percentage-Based Stride: The step size is a relative percentage (e.g., 10%, 25%) of the window length, ensuring the overlap scales with the window size.
To fully understand the results of our experiment, it is necessary to first explain the four evaluation methods employed.
3.5.1 Window Evaluation
This is the most direct evaluation method. In this approach, the ground truth itself is defined at the window level. For each audio file, we pre-process the ground truth by labeling any analysis window as "profanity" if its temporal overlap with a labeled profane word is 50% or greater.
The model then processes the audio, and each resulting classified window is directly compared against its corresponding ground-truth window label. Performance is then calculated using standard classification metrics (Accuracy, F1-Score, etc.) at the window level.

Figure 5 Window Evaluation infographic
3.5.2 Merged word Evaluation
This method aims to reconstruct a single "profanity event" from multiple, consecutive window predictions. It operates under the assumption that a single spoken profane word may be detected across several overlapping windows.
The process involves a post-processing step where consecutive windows that are predicted as the same class are merged into a single, continuous time segment as shown in Figure 6. For example:
• Ground Truth: A profane word "กู" exists from timestamp 2.00s to 3.25s.
• Window Predictions: Using a 0.5s window and 0.25s stride, the model might predict "profanity" for the windows: [2.00s-2.50s], [2.25s-2.75s], [2.50s-3.00s], and [2.75s-3.25s].
• Merged Instance: These four overlapping predictions are merged to form a single predicted profanity event from 2.00s to 3.25s.
This merged instance is then compared against the ground-truth word event to evaluate the model's ability to detect the complete duration of the profanity.

Figure 6 Merged word evaluation infographic
3.5.3 Merged word IoU Evaluation
This methodology enhances the word-merging approach by incorporating Intersection over Union (IoU) as the primary criterion for successful detection. As with the previous method, consecutive windows predicted as "profanity" are merged into a single predicted word instance.
However, the classification of this instance as a True Positive (TP) or False Positive (FP) is determined by its temporal overlap with a ground-truth word. Specifically, the IoU between the merged prediction and the ground-truth word is calculated. The prediction is only considered a TP if the IoU meets or exceeds a predefined threshold (e.g., 0.5).
A critical feature of this "separated" method is how it handles prediction gaps. If predictions are inconsistent and not contiguous, they are treated as distinct word instances, each evaluated independently against the ground truth with its own IoU score.
Example: Given ground truth: 2.00-3.25 seconds with word "กู", window size 0.5 seconds, stride 0.25 seconds: 
Scenario 1 - Consistent predictions: 
      • Predictions: 2.00-2.5s "กู", 2.25-2.75s "กู", 2.5-3.00s "กู", 2.75-3.25s "กู" 
      • Merged word: 2.00-3.25s "กู" 
      • Result: Perfect IoU = 1.0, classified as True Positive if IoU ≥ threshold (e.g., 0.5) 

Figure 7 Merged Word Evaluation using IoU threshold to help in classification decision if threshold greater than or equal to 50% of IoU it will classify as true positive then classify as false positive
Scenario 2 - Inconsistent predictions: 
      Predictions of [profanity, none, profanity] result in two separate instances. Each is independently compared to the ground truth, and each gets its own IoU score and TP/FP classification.
3.5.4 Merged word IoU Area Combination Evaluation
This evaluation method designed to assess performance from a region-centric perspective. It fundamentally differs from ordinary Merged word IoU Evaluation by evaluating all predictions associated with a single ground-truth instance collectively.

Instead of evaluating each fragmented prediction independently, this method first identifies a ground-truth profanity region. It then combines the total duration of all profanity predictions that fall within that region, even if they are discontinuous. A single, unified IoU is then computed for the entire ground-truth region.
Example: 
      • Predictions: 2.00-3.00s "กู", 3.00-4.00s "none", 4.00-5.00s "กู" 
      • Ground Truth: 2.00-5.00s "กู" 
      • Calculation: 
o Ground Truth span: [2.00-5.00] = 3.00s 
o Combined profanity predictions: [2.00-3.00] + [4.00-5.00] = 2.00s total coverage 
o Intersection = 2.00s (all profanity predictions fall within ground truth) 
o Union = 3.00s (full ground truth span, including the gap) 
o IoU = 2.00 / 3.00 = 0.67 (Assume IoU threshold is 0.5 then the result is 0.67 which is greater than 0.5 will count as True positive. If the result is lower or not equal to 0.5 will count as false positive.)

Figure 8 new Merged word Evaluation IoU Infographic
If the resulting IoU of 0.67 is greater than or equal to the 0.5 threshold, the detection for this entire ground-truth region is counted as a single True Positive. This method rewards models that can identify fragments of a profane word, even if they fail to capture it completely.
 3.5.5 Merged word IoU Area Addition Evaluation and Voice Activity Detection (VAD)

Figure 9 Merged word IoU Area Combination and Evaluation utilize with VAD
To enhance the temporal accuracy of profanity detection, we integrate a merged word-level Intersection over Union (IoU) evaluation approach with Voice Activity Detection (VAD). Our VAD implementation incorporates spectral gating for noise reduction, coupled with librosa-based energy thresholding at 20 dB to accurately segment speech regions. This methodology enables precise temporal localization of profanity instances by constraining predictions to acoustically active speech segments, thereby reducing false positive detections in non-speech regions. Moreover, this evaluation is most suitable for application usage.
Example: 
      Consider a case where the frame-level classifier produces overlapping predictions:
       Predictions: [2.00-3.00s "กู"], [3.00-4.00s "กู"] prediction threshold is 0.5
      Ground Truth: [2.50-3.50s "กู"]
      Step 1: Prediction Merging
      Consecutive predictions of the same class are merged into a single span:
      Merged Prediction: [2.00-4.00s "กู"]
      Step 2: VAD Integration
      Voice Activity Detection refines the prediction boundaries:
      VAD-refined span: [2.25-3.75s "กู"]
      Step 3: IoU Calculation
      For ground truth span GT = [2.50-3.50s] and prediction span P = [2.25-3.75s]:
- Intersection: max(2.50, 2.25) to min(3.50, 3.75) = [2.50-3.50s] = 1.00s
- Union: min(2.50, 2.25) to max(3.50, 3.75) = [2.25-3.75s] = 1.50s  
- IoU = |Intersection| / |Union| = 1.00s / 1.50s = 0.667
      Step 4: Classification Decision
      With IoU threshold τ = 0.5:
- Since IoU = 0.667 > 0.5 → True Positive
- Predicted class "กู" matches ground truth class → Correct classification
In conclusion, this research identifies two evaluation methodologies as the most logically rigorous for profanity detection assessment: Merged Word IoU Area Combination Evaluation and VAD-enhanced IoU Evaluation. The Merged Word IoU Area Combination approach provides optimal evaluation strictness by eliminating word duplication artifacts present in traditional Merged Word IoU Evaluation, while maintaining precise temporal overlap quantification. The VAD-enhanced IoU methodology offers superior practical applicability by constraining evaluation to acoustically active regions, thereby providing precise timestamp predictions that align with real-world deployment scenarios.

Chapter 5: Result
5.1 Model Performance
5.1.1 Evaluation Methodology Selection
Before presenting performance results, it is crucial to establish the most appropriate evaluation methodology. This research implemented and compared four distinct evaluation approaches:
1. Window Evaluation: Direct classification of individual audio windows
2. Merged Word Evaluation: Consolidation of overlapping window predictions into word decisions and timestmaps.
3. Merged Word IoU Evaluation: Word merged evaluation with IoU-based matching criteria
Critical Finding: Initial analysis revealed that Merged Word Evaluation produced artificially inflated F1 scores due to evaluation bias. This method only assessed the ground truth profane words, resulting in unrealistic precision.
Methodological Decision: Based on this analysis, Merged Word Combination IoU Evaluation and Merged word IoU were selected as the primary evaluation methodology for this research due to its:
• Objective temporal overlap quantification
• Absence of evaluation bias
• Reproducible and transparent metrics
• Alignment with established temporal detection literature
• Real-world usage implementation. (VAD)
5.1.2 Finding appropriate IoU Threshold

Figure 15 IoU Threshold line graph of Binary and Multiclass F1-score
Before conducting a comprehensive F1 score comparison across all evaluation methods, it was essential to establish an optimal Intersection over Union (IoU) threshold for our IoU-based evaluation methodologies. A threshold sensitivity analysis was performed to examine the relationship between the IoU threshold and the resulting F1 score. Figure 15 IoU Threshold line graph of Binary and Multiclass F1-score presents this analysis for both binary and multiclass classifications.
The analysis reveals a clear and expected inverse relationship: as the IoU threshold becomes stricter, requiring more precise temporal overlap to count as a positive detection, the F1 score consistently decreases. This occurs because higher thresholds make it more difficult to achieve a True Positive, thus lowering recall.
As shown in the figure, the peak F1 score for both binary (≈0.32) and multiclass (≈0.27) classification is achieved at the most lenient IoU threshold of 0.1. This indicates that while achieving precise localization is challenging, the model is effective at detecting at least a small portion of a profane utterance. A low threshold of 0.1 allows us to capture these partial detections, maximizing the F1 score by prioritizing detection sensitivity (recall).
Therefore, based on this analy sis, an IoU threshold of 0.1 was selected and is used for all subsequent IoU-based evaluations presented in this paper.

5.1.4 IoU percentage
This section presents the IoU percentages calculated from the Merged Word Evaluation (combined method).
According to this example 
Example: 
• Predictions: 2.00-3.00s "กู", 3.00-4.00s "none", 4.00-5.00s "กู" 
• Ground Truth: 2.00-5.00s "กู"
• Calculation:
o Ground Truth span: [2.00-5.00] = 3.00s
o Combined profanity predictions: [2.00-3.00] + [4.00-5.00] = 2.00s total coverage of Intersection = 2.00s (all profanity predictions fall within ground truth)
o Union = 3.00s (full ground truth span, including the gap)
o IoU = 2.00 / 3.00 = 0.67
This method may be fairer than the separated version, which evaluates words individually. From Figure 4, we observe that the highest IoU percentage is achieved with window 0.3s, stride 0.25 at 24.86 F1-Score by 0.05 or 0.21 (70%) at 25.34 F1-Score by percentage.

Chapter 6: Future Work and Limitation
6.1 Future Work
The findings from our comprehensive parameter analysis open several promising avenues for future research that can build upon this work.
6.1.1 Context-Aware Detection using Hierarchical Models
While our model operates on acoustic features, it lacks true linguistic understanding. A powerful improvement would be to integrate a second-stage language model. An acoustic model, optimized with the parameters from our study, could first generate a list of potential profanity "candidate" segments. These segments, along with their surrounding audio context, could then be fed into a large language model (LLM) to perform context-aware classification, helping to disambiguate between genuine profanity and innocuous uses of certain words
6.1.2 Advanced Boundary Refinement with Neural VAD
Our current system uses an energy-based Voice Activity Detector (VAD) via librosa to refine word boundaries. While effective, this method can be brittle in noisy environments. A significant improvement would be to replace this with a dedicated neural network-based VAD. Such a model could be trained to distinguish human speech from complex background noise (e.g., music, traffic) with much higher accuracy, leading to even more precise censoring that preserves surrounding audio integrity.
6.1.3 Real-Time Streaming Architecture
The current implementation processes audio files offline. A major engineering effort for future work would be to re-architect the system for real-time streaming audio processing. This would involve managing audio buffers, ensuring the detection pipeline can operate with low latency, and adapting the windowing/merging logic to function on an incomplete data stream. This would be essential for applications like live broadcast or communication platform monitoring.
6.2 Limitation
Sensitivity to Noise, the VAD-based boundary refinement is energy-based and can be unreliable in audio with significant background noise. Loud non-speech sounds can be incorrectly identified as speech, leading to imprecise censoring, while very quiet profanity might be missed entirely. Lack of True Linguistic, context as highlighted previously, the system is purely acoustic. The implementation of a functional censoring tool underscores this limitation, as it will censor a profane word regardless of the context in which it is used. It cannot distinguish between a genuine slur and a discussion about the word itself. The scarcity of datasets limits the model performance. Despite this, our model can detect and censor profanity to certain extent.
Chapter 7: Conclusion
This research presents a significant advancement in automated content moderation through the development of a Thai profanity detection system. Our implementation of a fine-tuned Wav2Vec 2.0 model, achieving 47.04% accuracy in multi-class classification, demonstrates the viability of automated profanity detection in Thai audio content. The system's comprehensive pipeline, from preprocessing to censoring, provides a practical solution for content moderation challenges. 
The project's key contributions include developing Thai language-specific profanity detection, find the appropriate evaluation methodologies, and implementation of effective windowing techniques for audio analysis. 
However, several challenges remain. The system’s performance is constrained by limitations in small datasets and sensitivity to background music noise. In case, we have larger dataset without collecting dataset on our own the model would be better. 
Future work should focus on expanding the training dataset, implementing more advanced data augmentation techniques. Additionally, the development of context-aware detection mechanisms and expand more Thai profanity word would enhance the system's practical utility. These improvements, combined with our current achievements, will contribute to the advancement of automated content moderation systems for diverse language environments.
